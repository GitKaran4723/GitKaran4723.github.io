### **1. Creating an Alphabetical Table**
To quickly solve alphabetical series questions, create this reference table:

| A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I | J | K | L | M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z | Y | X | W | V | U | T | S | R | Q | P | O | N |

- **Opposites**: This layout helps find opposite pairs (e.g., A–Z, B–Y).

## **Types of Alphabetical Series Patterns**

---

### **2. Simple Alphabetical Progression**
**Concept**: Letters follow a regular pattern (like skipping 1 letter).

**Example**:  
`A, C, E, G, I`  
→ +2 each step: A → C → E → G → I  
**Answer**: **I**

---

### **3. Series with Increasing Gaps**
**Concept**: Gaps between letters increase progressively.

**Example**:  
`A, B, D, G, K`  
→ A (+1) B (+2) D (+3) G (+4) K  
**Answer**: **K**

---

### **4. Fixed Gaps Series**
**Concept**: Constant gap between each letter.

**Example**:  
`B, E, H, K, N, Q`  
→ +3 at each step  
**Answer**: **Q**

---

### **5. Irregular Series Based on Reverse Positions**
**Concept**: Use alphabet table to find reverse pairings.

**Example**:  
`A Z, B Y, C X, D W`  
→ A–Z, B–Y, C–X... Opposite letters  
**Answer**: **W**

---

## **Special Series Based on Number Properties**

---

### **6. Prime Number Positions**
**Concept**: Letters at prime number positions (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13...)

**Example**:  
`B (2), C (3), E (5), G (7), K (11), M (13)`  
→ Positions: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13  
**Answer**: **M**

---

### **7. Vowel Series**
**Concept**: Letters are English vowels.

**Example**:  
`A, E, I, O, U`  
→ Vowels in order  
**Answer**: **U**

---

### **8. Square Number Positions**
**Concept**: Letters at positions of square numbers: 1², 2², 3²...

**Example**:  
`A (1), D (4), I (9), P (16), Y (25)`  
→ Squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25  
**Answer**: **Y**

---

### **9. Composite Number Series**
**Concept**: Letters at positions of composite numbers (not prime, >1)

**Example**:  
`D (4), F (6), H (8), J (10), L (12), N (14)`  
→ Composite positions  
**Answer**: **N**

## **Extra Practice Questions**

---

### **10. Series of Letter Pairs**
**Concept**: Pattern applies to two-letter combinations.

**Example**:  
`AM, BN, CO, DP, EQ, FR`  
→ First letters: A → B → C → D → E → F (+1)  
→ Second letters: M → N → O → P → Q → R (+1)  
**Answer**: **FR**

---

### **11. Alternate Skipping**
**Example**:  
`AB, DE, GH, JK, YN`  
→ AB (+3) DE (+3) GH...  
**Answer**: **JK**

---

## **Tips for Mastering Alphabetical Series**

1. **Create a reference table** with positions (1–26) and opposite letters.
2. **Look for patterns** involving:
   - Constant or increasing gaps
   - Vowels/consonants
   - Prime/square/composite number positions
3. **Reverse positions** can hint at mirrored patterns.
4. **Speed matters**: Try to decode the pattern within 15 seconds.
5. **Practice makes perfect**: The more types you solve, the faster you get.
