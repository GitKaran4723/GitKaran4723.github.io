### 📘 **Topic: Change in Percentage (Direct Formula)**

#### ✅ **Formula (Two Successive Changes)**  
If a quantity changes successively by **x%** and **y%**, then:  
\[
\textbf{Net % Change} = x + y + \frac{xy}{100}
\]  
- Use positive values for **increase**  
- Use negative values for **decrease**

---

### 🔢 **Example-Based Explanations**

#### 🧮 **Example: Two Increases**
- First increase = 20%, second increase = 10%
- Net change = \( 20 + 10 + \frac{20 \times 10}{100} = 32\% \)
- ✅ **Net increase: 32%**

---

#### 🛍 **Example: Two Successive Discounts**
- First discount = 20%, second discount = 30%
- Net change = \( -20 - 30 + \frac{(-20) \times (-30)}{100} = -44\% \)
- ✅ **Net decrease: 44%**

---

#### 💰 **Example: One Increase, One Decrease**
- Increase = 40%, then decrease = 20%
- Net change = \( 40 - 20 + \frac{40 \times (-20)}{100} = 12\% \)
- ✅ **Net increase: 12%**

---

### 👨‍👩‍👦 **Example: Percentage of a Percentage (Income Relation)**
- A’s income = 70% of B’s  
- B’s income = 50% of C’s  
- C’s income = ₹1,00,000  
\[
\text{A's Income} = 70\% \times 50\% \times 1,00,000 = \frac{70}{100} \times \frac{50}{100} \times 1,00,000 = ₹35,000
\]

---

### 📌 **Key Takeaways**
- Always apply the formula when **two successive percentage changes** are involved.
- Use **negative sign** for percentage **decrease/discount**.
- When finding a percentage of a percentage, **multiply the percentages directly**.
- This method is useful in **profit-loss, discounts, salary/income changes**, etc.
