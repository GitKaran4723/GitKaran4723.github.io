### **1. Multiplication Series**
**Concept**: In this type of series, each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a fixed number.

**How to Identify**: 
- Check if the numbers are increasing very rapidly.
- Try dividing a term by its previous term to find a common ratio.

**Example**:  
`3, 12, 48, 192, ?`  
→ 12 = 3 × 4  
→ 48 = 12 × 4  
→ 192 = 48 × 4  
**Next term** = 192 × 4 = **768**

---

### **2. Division Series**
**Concept**: Each number is divided by a number (which could be constant or follow a pattern).

**How to Identify**: 
- Numbers reduce drastically.
- Try dividing the current number by the next one or vice versa.

**Example**:  
`720, 120, 24, 6, ?`  
→ 720 ÷ 6 = 120  
→ 120 ÷ 5 = 24  
→ 24 ÷ 4 = 6  
**Next term** = 6 ÷ 3 = **2**

---

### **3. Fraction Multiplication Series**
**Concept**: Numbers increase at a slower rate through multiplication by a fraction.

**How to Identify**: 
- Numbers are increasing, but not as rapidly as in whole-number multiplication.
- Look for multiplication with fractions like 1.5 (3/2), 1.25 (5/4), etc.

**Example**:  
`32, 48, 72, ?`  
→ 32 × (3/2) = 48  
→ 48 × (3/2) = 72  
**Next term** = 72 × (3/2) = **108**

---

### **4. Square Number Series**
**Concept**: The terms are perfect squares of natural numbers.

**How to Identify**: 
- Check if each number has an integer square root.

**Example**:  
`1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?`  
→ These are squares of 1², 2², 3², 4², 5²  
**Next term** = 6² = **36**

---

### **5. Even Number Square Series**
**Concept**: Each term is the square of even numbers only.

**How to Identify**: 
- Square roots of the numbers are even integers.

**Example**:  
`0, 4, 16, 36, ?`  
→ 0², 2², 4², 6²  
**Next term** = 8² = **64**

---

### **6. Odd Number Addition Series**
**Concept**: Each term is obtained by adding consecutive odd numbers.

**How to Identify**: 
- The difference between consecutive terms forms a sequence of odd numbers: 3, 5, 7, etc.

**Example**:  
`1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?`  
→ Differences: 3, 5, 7, 9  
**Next term** = 25 + 11 = **36**

---

### **7. n² + 1 Series**
**Concept**: Each number follows the pattern `n² + 1`.

**How to Identify**: 
- Try subtracting 1 from each term and see if the result is a perfect square.

**Example**:  
`2, 5, 10, 17, 26, ?`  
→ 1² + 1 = 2  
→ 2² + 1 = 5  
→ 3² + 1 = 10  
→ 4² + 1 = 17  
→ 5² + 1 = 26  
**Next term** = 6² + 1 = **37**

---

### **8. Even Number Addition Series**
**Concept**: Each term is formed by adding consecutive even numbers.

**How to Identify**: 
- Differences between terms follow even number pattern: 2, 4, 6, 8, etc.

**Example**:  
`0, 2, 6, 12, 20, ?`  
→ Differences: 2, 4, 6, 8  
**Next term** = 20 + 10 = **30**

---

### **9. Cube Number Series**
**Concept**: The series consists of perfect cubes.

**How to Identify**: 
- Check if each number has an integer cube root.

**Example**:  
`1, 8, 27, 64, 125, ?`  
→ 1³, 2³, 3³, 4³, 5³  
**Next term** = 6³ = **216**

---

### **10. Cube + 1 Series**
**Concept**: Each number is of the form `n³ + 1`.

**How to Identify**: 
- Subtract 1 from each number and check if it's a cube.

**Example**:  
`2, 9, 28, 65, ?`  
→ 1³ + 1 = 2  
→ 2³ + 1 = 9  
→ 3³ + 1 = 28  
→ 4³ + 1 = 65  
**Next term** = 5³ + 1 = **126**

---

### **Tips for Solving Number Series Quickly**
1. **Identify the pattern in under 10–15 seconds**.
2. **Sharp increase/decrease**? Think multiplication or division.
3. **Gradual increase**? Try checking differences.
4. **Differences forming a pattern**? Could be addition of consecutive numbers (odd/even).
5. **Use the unit digit** for multiplication-based series to narrow possibilities.
6. **Practice regularly**—familiarity with patterns makes you faster.
