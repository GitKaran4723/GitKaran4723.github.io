## 📘 **Percentage Applications – Detailed Notes**

---

### ✅ **1. Discounts**

#### 📌 **Concept**:  
A **discount** is a reduction in the marked price (MRP) of a product, usually expressed as a percentage.

#### 🧠 **Formula**:
```text
Discount = (Discount% × Marked Price) ÷ 100  
Selling Price = Marked Price – Discount
```

#### 📝 Example 1:
**T-shirt MRP** = ₹1000, **Discount** = 20%  
→ Discount = 1000 × (20/100) = ₹200  
→ **Selling Price** = 1000 – 200 = ₹800

#### 📝 Example 2:
**Shirt MRP** = ₹2500, **Discount** = 30%  
→ Discount = 2500 × (30/100) = ₹750  
→ **Selling Price** = 2500 – 750 = ₹**1750**

---

### ✅ **2. Understanding Percentages Visually**

#### 📌 **Concept**:
- **1%** means 1 part out of 100  
- **50%** = half of the quantity  
- **100%** = the full value  
- **More than 100%** = more than the original (e.g., 250% of something is 2.5×)

#### 📝 Example:
**250% of 250**  
→ (250 × 250) ÷ 100 = **625**

---

### ✅ **3. Converting Percentages to Decimals and Fractions**

#### 📌 **Conversions**:

| Percentage | Decimal Form | Fraction Form |
|------------|--------------|----------------|
| 50%        | 0.50         | 1/2            |
| 75%        | 0.75         | 3/4            |
| 160%       | 1.60         | 8/5            |

#### 🧠 Tips:
- Divide % by 100 to get decimal:  
  → 160% = 160 ÷ 100 = 1.60  
- For fractions, simplify the result:  
  → 75% = 75/100 = 3/4

---

### ✅ **4. Calculating Percentage of a Value**

#### 📌 **Formula**:
```text
(Percentage × Number) ÷ 100
```

#### 📝 Examples:
- **10% of 10** = (10 × 10)/100 = **1**
- **20% of 100** = (20 × 100)/100 = **20**

---

#### 🧮 **Marks Percentage Example**:
If you score 40 out of 80:
→ (40/80) × 100 = **50%**

---

### ✅ **5. Comparing Percentages**

#### 📌 **Concept**:
Find actual values using the given percentages and compare.

#### 📝 Example:
- **25% of 400** = (25 × 400)/100 = 100  
- **30% of 300** = (30 × 300)/100 = 90  
→ **Greater value** = **25% of 400**

---

### ✅ **6. Percentage Increase**

#### 📌 **Formula**:
```text
Increased Value = Original Value × (1 + %Increase/100)
```

#### 📝 Example:
**Increase 200 by 10%**  
→ New Value = 200 × (1 + 10/100) = 200 × 1.10 = **220**
