Unit 1 Overview

1. WHAT’S DESIGN THINKING?

Definition: Design Thinking is a problem-solving methodology that focuses on understanding the user, challenging assumptions, and redefining problems to create innovative solutions.

2. Why do we need design thinking?

Purpose:

  • User-Centric Solutions: Design Thinking emphasizes understanding and addressing the needs of the end-users.
  • Innovation: It fosters a creative and iterative approach to problem-solving.
  • Adaptability: Design Thinking allows for flexibility and adaptability based on user feedback.

3. What makes design thinking unique?

Characteristics:

  • Empathy: Understanding the user's perspective and needs.
  • Iterative Process: Embracing a cycle of prototyping, testing, and refining.
  • Collaboration: Involving multidisciplinary teams for diverse insights.
  • Human-Centered: Placing the user at the center of the design process.
  • Bias Toward Action: Focusing on implementing solutions rather than extensive planning.

4. DESIGN ACTION PLAN:

Characteristics of Action Plan:

  • Clear Objectives: Clearly defined goals and outcomes.
  • Measurable Targets: Establishing specific, measurable criteria for success.
  • Timeline: Setting a realistic and structured timeline for each phase.
  • Resource Allocation: Identifying and allocating necessary resources.
  • Evaluation: Regularly assessing progress and adjusting the plan as needed.

5. Empathize Phase:

Explanation: This phase involves understanding the user's experiences and feelings. Designers immerse themselves in the user's environment to gain insights into their needs and challenges.

6. Define Phase:

Explanation: In this phase, the design team synthesizes the information gathered in the empathize phase to define the core problems and challenges. This includes creating a user persona and clearly articulating the problem statement.

7. Ideate Phase:

Explanation: Teams generate a wide range of creative ideas without judgment. The emphasis is on quantity and diversity, encouraging out-of-the-box thinking and innovation.

8. Prototype Phase:

Explanation: Selected ideas from the ideation phase are translated into tangible, visual representations or prototypes. These prototypes are simple, low-cost versions that allow for quick testing and iteration.

9. Test Phase:

Explanation: Prototypes are tested with real users to gather feedback. This phase helps identify what works and what needs improvement. The feedback loop guides further iterations and refinements.
