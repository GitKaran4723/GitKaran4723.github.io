Socio-technical Systems:

1. Complex Systems:

Definition: Systems characterized by a large number of interconnected elements or components, often with nonlinear interactions, making them difficult to understand and predict.

2. System Engineering:

Definition: An interdisciplinary approach to design, implement, and manage complex systems throughout their life cycles, involving various engineering disciplines and focusing on the system as a whole.

3. System Procurement:

Definition: The process of acquiring or purchasing a system from external sources, involving activities such as defining requirements, evaluating vendors, and negotiating contracts.

4. System Development:

Definition: The process of creating or enhancing a system, including activities such as design, coding, testing, and documentation.

5. System Operation:

Definition: The phase in the system life cycle where the system is deployed and actively used to fulfill its intended purpose.

Dependability and Security:

6. Dependability Properties:

Definition: The set of attributes that characterize the trustworthiness and reliability of a system, including availability, reliability, safety, and security.

7. Availability and Reliability:

Availability: The proportion of time a system is operational and accessible.

Reliability: The ability of a system to perform its functions without failures over time.

8. Safety:

Definition: The assurance that a system operates without causing harm to users, the environment, or other systems.

9. Security:

Definition: The protection of a system's assets, including data, software, and hardware, from unauthorized access, attacks, or damage.

Requirements Engineering:

10. Functional and Non-functional Requirements:

Functional Requirements: Specify what the system must do, the features it must have, and the services it must provide.

Non-functional Requirements: Define qualities or constraints of the system, such as performance, usability, or security.

11. The Software Requirements Document:

Definition: A formal document that details the functional and non-functional requirements of the software system.

12. Requirements Specification:

Definition: The process of translating collected requirements into a formal and structured document that serves as a blueprint for system development.

13. Requirements Engineering Processes:

  • Elicitation and Analysis: Gathering and understanding requirements from stakeholders.
  • Specification: Documenting requirements in a clear and unambiguous manner.
  • Validation: Ensuring that requirements are complete, consistent, and meet stakeholders' needs.
  • Management: Tracking and controlling changes to requirements throughout the project.

14. Requirements Elicitation and Analysis:

Elicitation: Techniques for extracting requirements from stakeholders.

Analysis: Understanding and refining collected requirements for clarity and feasibility.

15. Requirements Validation:

Definition: Ensuring that the documented requirements are accurate, complete, and aligned with stakeholders' expectations.

16. Requirements Management:

Definition: The systematic process of managing changes to requirements, ensuring traceability and addressing potential conflicts.
